## Cross-cutting indexes (by game-design relevance)

These point at which chapters are richest for each kind of material. Not exhaustive — use as a starting point, then grep the specific files.

### First-person testimonies (strongest character seeds)
- **02** Liverpool-Irish ex-railway labourer driven to bone-picking after being cheated out of his savings.
- **03** 60-year-old pure-finder widow (buried 8 children, husband drowned ex-sailor). Also: Manchester cotton-clerk ruined by drink.
- **06** The Naples diver "the Fish" (comparative anecdote — web-footed, lost diving for the King of Two Sicilies' silver cup).
- **07** Sewer-hunter testimony on gang life, tides, rats, and the underground geography.
- **08** 13-year-old mud-lark boy; Irishwoman mud-lark.
- **10** Multiple dialect-heavy dustmen and sifters (e.g. "Scratchey Jack").

### Vocabulary / slang glossary candidates
- "tosh" / "toshers" — ch. 07 (copper scrap; sewer-hunters themselves)
- "pure" — ch. 03 (dogs' dung; origin of term in leather-dressing)
- "bunters" — ch. 03 (older female rag/pure collectors)
- "straw-yards" — ch. 02 (winter night-asylums for the destitute)
- "trampers" — ch. 02 (seasonal country labourers wintering as street-finders)
- "shore-men" / "shore-workers" — ch. 07 (sewer-hunter self-designation)
- "small master" — ch. 06 (self-employed dredgerman)
- "brieze" — ch. 10 (sifted dust sold to brickmakers)
- "running dustmen" — ch. 10 (unlicensed collectors)
- "chiffoniers" — ch. 02 (Paris rag-gatherers; class-ranked meeting rooms)

### Economic data (prices, wages, scale)
- **02** Rag/bone/metal prices per lb; grubber earnings 6–8d/day; annual trade volume 3.7M lb bones, 1.24M lb rags.
- **03** Pure: 8d–1s 2d per bucket; historic high 3s 6d–4s; ~200–250 finders; ~£2,600 annual total earnings.
- **06** Dredger earnings; corpse-reward economics.
- **09** Per-parish counts of sweeps/dustmen/nightmen; borough housing/population tables.
- **10** London dust trade aggregate: capital ~£28,590; income ~£147,700; expenditure ~£75,400; profit ~£72,300 (~100%); ~90 contractors, ~1,800 labourers, ~600 horse-carts.

### Locations (London geography anchors)
- Bermondsey tanyards (ch. 03, 10) — Leomont & Roberts, Bavingtons, Beech, Murrell, Cheeseman, Powell, Jones, Jourdans, Kent, Moorcroft, Davis.
- Rosemary Lane / Petticoat Lane / Ragfair / St Katherine's Docks district (ch. 02, 03) — "head-quarters of the bone-grubbers."
- Ratcliff Highway, Commercial Rd, Mile End Rd (ch. 04) — cigar-end beats.
- Thames foreshore Shadwell/Wapping/Surrey side (ch. 06, 07, 08).
- Friar-street, Southwark Bridge-road (ch. 01) — bone-grinding works.
- Whitechapel workhouse hospital (ch. 02).

### Atmosphere / sensory detail (worldbuilding)
- ch. 02 — the "wretched locality" between the Docks and Rosemary Lane (dust-heaps, ragged children, tumbled houses).
- ch. 03 — the widow's bare room with rag-stuffed window panes.
- ch. 07 — the underground geography: iron tide-doors, rotten brick, branching sewers, foul-air chambers, tidal sluice-surges, rat swarms.
- ch. 08 — the foreshore at low tide.
- ch. 10 — the dust-yard: sifting gangs, piles, "brieze," the contractor's scale.
